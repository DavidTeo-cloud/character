About the event

Character is learned, and the family is where it starts

Since the early 1990s, Unificationist educators and clergy have built character education curricula, marriage-preparation courses and family ministries in classrooms and congregations from Moscow to Chicago. Next Step brings that experience together in one room.

We will study the core ideas behind this work: that lasting love rests on mature character, that the home is the first school of love, and that a good life is lived for the sake of others. We will also look honestly at the history, including programs that met resistance, and talk about how to share these values well in today's schools, churches and families.

Core themes

What we will explore together

01

Character as the foundation of love

Mature character joins moral feeling, moral knowing and moral action. Without it, even strong feelings can do harm.

02

The family as the school of love

We learn to love as children, siblings, spouses and parents. Those four relationships shape how we treat everyone else.

03

Living for the sake of others

True love is other-centered. Kindness, fidelity and forgiveness carry relationships when feelings fade.

04

Preparing for marriage

Integrity, purity before marriage, communication and fair conflict resolution are skills young people can learn.

Background

Thirty years of character and family education

  1. 1990

    The International Educational Foundation (IEF) is founded to advocate character education and family-strengthening education, beginning in the former Soviet Union.

  2. 1992

    More than 1,500 Russian educators meet in a conference held under the Russian Ministry of Education. IEF's My Journey in Life curriculum, which IEF says reached more than 10,000 schools, grows out of this work.

  3. 1996

    The True Family Values Ministry is founded in Chicago on December 27 and begins training clergy to teach marriage and family values in their own congregations.

  4. 1999–2000

    Volunteers with the Pure Love Alliance teach the CLUE curriculum (Creating Love and Uplifting Esteem) in up to 61 Chicago public schools, until the district ends their access over curriculum approval.

  5. 2000

    The American Clergy Leadership Conference (ACLC) is founded in May, when 120 clergy from 17 denominations gather in Seoul. True Family Values is one of its three pillars.

  6. 2026

    The True Family Values Ministry marks its 30th anniversary. Next Step asks what the coming decades should look like.
